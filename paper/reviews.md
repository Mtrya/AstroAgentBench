# Reviews

## Review 1

### Paper Summary:
Benchmark for LLM agents on space mission planning. Seven task families (scheduling, observation, constellation design, relay), 5 test cases each, with physics-based verifiers checking feasibility and solution quality. Five agent systems tested. Also has failure mode analysis and ablations on procedure injection / memory.

### Summary Of Strengths:
The verifier-backed evaluation is by far the best thing here. Having actual physical constraint checkers (orbital mechanics, slew, battery, link budget) means you can't fake a good score with a plausible-sounding plan. I wish more benchmarks did this.

The failure mode taxonomy in Section 5.1 is useful. Task-formulation vs solution-construction is a distinction I hadn't seen before and it applies beyond this domain. The "Private World" example where Claude invents its own task specification is kind of hilarious and very illustrative.

Seven different task families is a lot of work. I respect the engineering effort.

### Summary Of Weaknesses:
Why is this at ACL? I keep coming back to this question and the paper never answers it. The benchmark is about orbital mechanics, SGP4 propagation, constellation geometry. The fact that LLMs are the agents doesn't make it an NLP contribution. This should be at ICAPS or an aerospace venue.

35 test cases total. You can't rank five systems from five data points per condition. I don't believe any of the specific numerical rankings in Table 3 and the paper offers no confidence intervals to convince me otherwise.

The solver baselines are all over the place. Some families have near-optimal solvers, others use simple heuristics. So when an agent gets 80% on one family and 50% on another, I dont know if thats because the tasks are harder or the reference solver is stronger. Makes cross-family comparisons pretty meaningless.

### Comments Suggestions And Typos:
Way too long. Cut the related work and move per-family baselines to appendix.
What does a score of 58.45 practically mean? I couldn't figure this out from the normalization description.
"specialised" and "specialized" both appear. Pick one.

## Review 2

### Paper Summary:
This paper introduces AstroReason-Bench, a benchmark for executable space mission planning under physical and operational constraints. The benchmark evaluates LLM-based agent systems that produce structured planning artifacts, which are automatically checked by an external verifier. The paper further evaluates multiple agent systems under a fixed execution budget and compares their outputs with classical optimization-based baselines.

### Summary Of Strengths:
This paper introduces a benchmark for executable space mission planning where submitted artifacts generated by LLM-based agents are evaluated by an external verifier. The evaluation is fully automatic and does not involve manual intervention or post-hoc repair.
This paper provides empirical analysis of agent performance, including failure mode discussion based on trace-level observations, as well as studies of verifier usage, procedure injection, and memory accumulation.

### Summary Of Weaknesses:
This paper is primarily a benchmark construction and evaluation study, without proposing new methods or algorithms. The contribution is therefore limited to dataset and evaluation design.
The comparison includes multiple agents that differ in underlying models as well as system-level configurations such as prompting strategies and tool-use setups. As a result, it may be difficult to clearly disentangle which component contributes most to the observed performance differences. It would be helpful if the authors could provide additional controlled experiments to better isolate the effect of individual factors such as model choice, prompting, and system scaffolding.

## Review 3

### Paper Summary:
This paper presents AstroReason-Bench, a benchmark for testing LLM agents on space mission planning tasks. It includes 7 task families covering scheduling, observation planning and constellation design, with an independent verifier that checks both plan feasibility and actual mission value. The authors evaluate 5 mainstream LLM agent systems against specialized solver baselines, and analyze common failure modes as well as the effects of procedure injection and memory accumulation.

### Summary Of Strengths:
It expands agent benchmarks from common web/coding scenarios into physics-constrained planning, filling a clear gap in current testbeds.
The two-layer validity and quality evaluation is rigorous. It avoids rewarding outputs that follow the format but deliver no real mission value.
The trace-level failure analysis provides practical insights for building better planning agents.
### Summary Of Weaknesses:
The experimental scope is too limited. There are only five test cases per task and five evaluated agent systems. API cost is not a strong justification for this scale. Locally deployed open-source models could be included to expand the evaluation. In addition, the tested systems are mostly common end-user CLI tools, and the benchmark misses several notable, recently proposed agent frameworks from research literature. This limits the generalizability of findings about agentic planning performance.
The benchmark has a clear gap with real engineering complexity. It simplifies many physical and operational constraints in actual space missions.
